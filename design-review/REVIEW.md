# FLUX: visual and UX design review

Scope: what the app looks like and how it feels to use. Physics is out of scope. No app code was changed.

**How this was looked at.** `design-review/capture.mjs` drives the running app (`npm start`) in headless
Chromium at 1440×900, 1280×720, 1024×768 and 390×844 (phone, touch) and writes every screenshot cited
here to `design-review/shots/`. Every lab × scenario was also shot at 1440×900 (the same coverage as
`scripts/gallery.mjs`, with real fonts) and reviewed as contact sheets. Only the five gallery frames the
review cites are kept in `shots/gallery/`; `node design-review/capture.mjs gallery` regenerates the rest.
Every screenshot was opened and looked at. Coordinates are in screenshot pixels (x from left, y from top).
`coverage.json` holds the measured share of the viewport the HUD covers, and `words.json` the words per
HUD region. Both are regenerated by `capture.mjs coverage words`.

Caveats: (1) This sandbox's proxy breaks Google Fonts inside Chromium. The capture script serves the same
Inter and JetBrains Mono files locally (`FLUX_FONTS=…`), so the type is what a student sees. On a normal
machine the variable isn't needed. (2) WebGL here is software-rendered at DPR 1. Antialiasing is rougher
than on a real laptop, but layout, color and overlap are exact. (3) A native `<select>` can't be
screenshotted open.

---

## 1. Verdict

FLUX has an excellent core: honest physics you can touch, math typeset properly, and a practice sheet
that on a phone is better than most commercial ones. But the screen is laid out for the person who built it.
Five floating panels plus a hint bar cover 35–46% of a laptop screen and 52–60% of a Chromebook screen. The
camera ignores them, so the object, the probe or the inset the lab is *about* often sits under a panel.
The number strip at the bottom collapses to "1.1…" at 1024 px and repeats the equation panel at every size.
Practice, arguably half the app, is a small yellow pill that opens a side panel while the whole lab stays
live and competes for attention. That includes during a timed exam. Color means too many things. Nearly
every label, hint and rule is said twice. None of this is deep. It's mostly layout and discipline, and the
parts worth keeping are clear.

## 2. Top 10 problems, ranked by impact on a student

### 1. Practice is bolted onto the lab instead of being a mode of its own
- **What's wrong.** The entry to half the app is a small pill with the same size, color and weight as
  **Units** (`shots/first-load-1440.png`, header row y≈184: *Practice* and *Units* side by side, both yellow).
  Opening it swaps the equation panel for a 384-px column. The full lab stays live on the right, *including
  its own yellow primary button*. In `shots/problem-symbol-box-1440.png` the student's **Check** (x 33–124,
  y 790–826) competes with **Play ∫ dV** (x 1139–1409, y 664–704), a bigger yellow button in the same
  style. During a **timed practice exam** the Setup panel stays editable
  (`shots/exam-in-progress-1440.png`: +Charge, −Charge, Delete, coordinates and the test-charge slider are
  all live at x 1139–1419), and it can show a lab that has nothing to do with the question.
  `shots/exam-in-progress-1280.png` shows "Where the field vanishes, from V(x)" next to a point-charge
  scene full of grey veil boxes, and `shots/exam-problem-2-1280.png` shows "A charged sphere accelerated
  through ΔV" next to the same point-charge scene. At 1280×720 the problem list shows **one** problem
  above the fold (`shots/practice-list-chapter-1280.png`, the first row at y≈581; three rows of chips and
  buttons sit above it).
- **Why it matters.** The night before an exam, practice *is* the product. The layout says it's a sidebar.
  Two primary buttons split attention at the moment of answering, and an exam with a live sandbox beside
  it isn't an exam.
- **Fix.** Make it a top-level switch in the header: `Explore | Practice` (a segmented control, not a pill).
  In Practice mode, the problem sheet becomes the main column (about 480 px, or centered on a phone). The lab
  becomes a *viewer*: Setup collapses into a closed **Lab controls** drawer that opens after the problem
  is solved, the equation panel and readout are hidden, and the scene reframes into the free space. In
  exam mode, show the scene only for problems that load one (`tpl.sim`), with no controls at all. Move the
  list's filter chips behind one **Filter** button so problems start at the top of the list.
- **Effort.** L

### 2. The readout strip truncates to nonsense, and it duplicates the equation panel anyway
- **What's wrong.** At 1024 wide, the four readout cells read **"1.1…", "1…", "9…", "+.."**
  (`shots/first-load-1024.png`, y≈680–705) and **"6.0…", "1…", "0…", "0…"**
  (`shots/busy-interference-P-1024.png`, y≈700). Even at 1440: **"1.13×10⁵ N·m…"** and **"+1.00 μC · 1 i…"**
  (`shots/first-load-1440.png`, x 617–1090, y≈833). At 1280: **"V is a sc…"**
  (`shots/problem-solved-labvals-1280.png`, x 835–925, y≈654). Every value it shows is already in the
  equation panel. In `shots/capacitor-default-1280.png` the readout's C, V, q and U (y≈654) repeat live rows
  3, 4, 5 and 8 on the left. The cause is `.readout { left: 416px; right: 332px }`, which leaves 276 px for
  four cells at 1024.
- **Why it matters.** A Chromebook is a named target device. At that width the most prominent number
  strip on screen shows ellipses, and a stressed student reads "9…" as a value.
- **Fix.** Remove the readout on desktop. Promote one or two headline values ("the answer this lab is
  about") into a larger **result row** at the top of the equation panel, under the law. If the strip stays,
  let the cells wrap onto a second line, drop to two cells below 1280, and never ellipsize a number.
- **Effort.** S (remove) to M (result row in every lab)

### 3. The scene is treated as the background: panels cover 35–60% of it, and the camera doesn't know
- **What's wrong.** Measured union of visible HUD boxes (`coverage.json`): **35–46% at 1440 and 1280, and
  52–60% at 1024**. The camera still centers the lab on the full viewport, so the content lands under panels.
  - `shots/busy-lenses-micro-1024.png`: the **object and the objective lens are hidden under the
    equation panel**. Only the eyepiece (x≈565) is visible. The label "final (virtual)" is stranded at
    x 305–385, y≈665, next to the readout.
  - `shots/busy-interference-P-1024.png`: the **"What arrives at P" inset**, which the README makes this
    lab's point, sits **under the brand header** (x≈237–425, y≈0–130). The slits are under the equation
    panel.
  - `shots/busy-field-default-1440.png`: the default **probe P and its "|E| = 37.0…" label** sit at the
    left edge of Setup (x≈1060–1125, y≈300–350) and are clipped by it, and its E arrow pokes out past Setup's
    right edge (x≈1425, y≈265).
  - `shots/busy-magforce-helix-1440.png`: "B = 200.00 mT ↑" floats at x 1280–1415, y≈152, touching the top
    of Setup, far from any B arrow.
  - `shots/review-due-2-1440.png`: the circuit's third resistor label "R₃ = … I₃ = …" is cut in half by
    Setup's left edge (x 1070–1125, y 420–480).
- **Why it matters.** The 3D scene is what nothing else offers. Hiding the object, the probe or the inset
  defeats the lab, and the student can't tell anything is missing.
- **Fix.** Dock the panels into fixed columns and give the renderer the free rectangle
  (`camera.setViewOffset` or a CSS-sized canvas), so each lab's framing centers in the free area. Keep
  scene insets (the wave inset, the spectrum ruler) inside that rectangle. Panels still overlay a little,
  but no longer over the subject.
- **Effort.** M

### 4. "Predict first" is below the fold almost everywhere
- **What's wrong.** The card is the last thing in a scrolling Setup panel. Measured at load
  (`capture.mjs` log): at **1280×720** the card starts at y=618 and Setup ends at y=656, so only the
  header row shows (`shots/capacitor-default-1280.png`, y≈640: "▾ Predict first 0/3 right"). At **1024** it's
  the header plus one line. On the **phone** it starts at y=984, *below the bottom of an 844-px screen*,
  inside a sheet that's 203 px tall (`shots/first-load-390.png`: not visible at all). Even at 1440 it's cut
  off in the labs with more controls: Interference (`shots/busy-interference-P-1440.png`, y≈830, header
  only), Diffraction and Thin film (`shots/busy-diffraction-rayleigh-1440.png`,
  `shots/busy-thinfilm-soap-1440.png`), and Circuits' three-branch layout
  (`shots/busy-circuits-threebranch-1440.png`, the "Try it" button clipped at y≈833).
- **Why it matters.** 53 carefully written experiments aimed at the classic wrong answers, and most
  students will never scroll to them.
- **Fix.** Promote it: a one-line prompt at the *top* of Setup ("Predict: pull the plates to 2d. What
  happens to C? ▸"), expanded on a lab's first visit and collapsed after. Or a card over the scene's
  lower-left corner. While a prediction is active, collapse the other Setup controls so the whole card fits.
- **Effort.** S–M

### 5. Hidden and locked states look broken, not hidden
- **What's wrong.**
  - Veiled scene labels render as **empty grey bars** floating in 3D: the bar above P at x 597–767,
    y 182–203 and about eight chips on the ring (`shots/problem-principle-step-1280.png`), and grey boxes
    scattered around the charge during the exam (`shots/exam-in-progress-1280.png`, x≈420–830,
    y≈250–500). They read as rendering glitches.
  - The **locked numeric box** is the same box at 45% opacity. Its placeholder "e.g. 2.5e-6" works out to
    about **2.0:1** contrast, with no lock icon, and the reason sits under it in 11-px dim text
    (`shots/problem-symbol-box-1024.png`, x 33–341, y 592–645).
  - After a miss, the key's answer is shown as **raw parser syntax**, "Answer: k*Q/sqrt(a^2 + y^2)"
    (`shots/missed-gap-answered-1440.png`, y≈242), in a panel where everything else is typeset.
  - Under an **empty** answer box come "Answer: 45200 V" and then "LAB 45200 V **agrees**" in green
    (`shots/missed-what-went-wrong-1440.png`, y 265–290). It agrees with the key, not with anything the
    student entered, but it reads like praise for an answer they got wrong.
- **Why it matters.** "Is it broken?" is the worst question to plant in a stressed student's head.
  Locked states have to *announce* themselves.
- **Fix.** Veil = a small pill with a lock glyph ("🔒 hidden") in the label's place, plus one line in the
  problem banner: "Values in the scene are hidden until you solve it." Locked box: a visible lock row
  ("🔒 Unlocks when the formula above is right") at text-muted contrast, not a 45% ghost. Typeset the key's
  formula the way the preview already does. Reword the lab row to "Lab: 45 200 V (matches the key)",
  and show it only after the student has an answer to compare.
- **Effort.** S

### 6. One color, many meanings: yellow alone has about 27
- **What's wrong.** See the table in §5.1. Yellow is: the Practice and Units buttons, the active exam
  tab, the active-lab underline, every slider thumb, every primary button, insight titles, the Predict
  title, the principle-step border, hint text, the solution heading, the focus ring, *and* in the scene,
  current, the E field at a probe, capacitor E arrows, outward flux, the selected charge, the net force,
  the intermediate image, a polarizer beam, the AC current phasor, and the sum wave. Green is "correct",
  "mastered", "resistance", "no breakdown", "near resonance", "real image", "visible band" and "headline
  value". **The same quantity changes color between panels:** Δy is blue in the equation panel and green in
  the readout (`shots/busy-interference-P-1440.png`, eq panel y≈415 vs readout x 435, y≈833), and θ_min is
  blue vs green (`shots/busy-diffraction-rayleigh-1440.png`, y≈389 vs x 435, y≈833). Red and blue are ±
  charge, but also vector **a** and vector **b** (`shots/gallery/e1-vectors-345.png`), and wire potential
  runs blue→orange in Circuits.
- **Why it matters.** Once a color stops meaning one thing, the student stops reading color at all.
  That defeats the scene's main encoding: which way is the field, which way is the current, is this right.
- **Fix.** Write a semantic palette and hold to it (a proposal follows the table in §5.1). Status colors (green right, red
  wrong) are used *only* for grading. Yellow in the UI means "the one thing to press". In the scene, a
  quantity keeps one color in every lab (E, B, I, F, v). Readout and equation values take the color of their
  scene object or none, never "ok green".
- **Effort.** M

### 7. Navigation: bare numerals, a header that changes height, five names for one unit, and a first screen from two weeks ago
- **What's wrong.**
  - The exam tabs are **"1 2 3 4 5 6 7 W"** with the names only in a hover `title`
    (`shots/first-load-1440.png`, y≈99). The phone picker is no better: its options are "Exam 1 … Exam 7,
    Waves" (`shots/phone-exam-select-390.png`, x 122–216, y 8–40; options read from the DOM).
  - In Exam 4 the lab tabs **wrap to two lines**, "Biot–/Savart" and "Mag/force"
    (`shots/busy-circuits-twoloop-1440.png`, x 186–355, y 130–162). The header grows from 196 to 214 px
    (measured, `coverage.json`) and the equation panel shifts down 17 px compared with
    `shots/first-load-1440.png`. In a review session that crosses exams, this happens between consecutive
    problems (§4.5).
  - The last unit is called **W** (tab), **Waves** (phone picker), **Wave optics** (README), **"Exam
    final"** (subtitle, `shots/busy-diffraction-rayleigh-1440.png`, y≈60: "PHY 2049 · Exam final · Ch
    63–65") and **"Final exam"** (practice kicker).
  - With no hash, the app opens **Exam 2 · Gauss** (`shots/first-load-1440.png`). That's hardcoded in
    `router.js:16`, though `catalog.js` stores exam dates: Exam 2 was 9/11, and on 9/26 the next exam is
    Exam 4 (10/9). The dates are never used or shown.
  - **Integrals** appears under Exam 2 and Exam 3 with nothing to say it's the same lab in a different
    mode, and the Exam 3 version contradicts itself. `shots/gallery/e3-integral-ring.png` shows the law
    V = kQ/√(a²+y²) at the top, but the insight below it still explains the *field*: "|dE| is constant…
    the axial pieces add: E_y = kQy/(y²+a²)^{3/2}" (x 33–360, y 395–585). Its Predict card asks about
    "the rod's charge per length λ" while a ring is on screen (x 1139–1409, y 737–790). Compare
    `shots/gallery/e2-integral-ring.png`: same tab name, different law, same insight.
- **Why it matters.** Orientation is the first 10 seconds. "Which exam am I in, and is this what's on
  Friday?" should never take a hover.
- **Fix.** Replace the numeral strip with an exam picker that shows name and date ("Exam 4 · Circuits &
  magnetism · Fri 10/9 ▾") on every device. Default to the next upcoming exam. Keep lab tabs on one line
  (nowrap, with a scroll shadow if needed) and rename "Mag force" to "B-force" or "Magnetic force" to fit.
  Use one name for the last unit ("Wave optics · final"). Label the Exam 3 tab "Integrals (V)", or add a
  line in its insight: "Same lab as Exam 2, in ∫dV mode."
- **Effort.** S

### 8. Too much to read before acting, and most of it is said twice
- **What's wrong.** Word counts on screen at 1440, counting math as text (`words.json`): **285
  (Field) to 571 (AC) words per lab**. The insight box runs up to 191 words (Thin film), and Setup's help
  prose runs up to 74 words in 10.5–11-px dim text (`shots/busy-thinfilm-soap-1440.png`, x 1139–1409,
  y 600–720). Duplicates on every lab screen:
  - The Setup subtitle **and** the hint bar both say "Drag a charge through the surface"
    (`shots/first-load-1440.png`, y≈223 and y≈877).
  - "1 square = 12.5 cm" appears in Setup and in the hint bar (`shots/problem-principle-step-1280.png`).
  - The readout repeats the equation panel (problem 2).
  - The magforce color key is written as a sentence in Setup ("Teal arrows: B. Purple: E. White: v. Gold: F.")
    (`shots/busy-magforce-helix-1440.png`, y 537–570) instead of a legend.

  The magforce key isn't alone: Lenses' insight decodes its rays in prose ("Gold: parallel, then through
  F. Teal: straight through the centre. Blue: …", `shots/gallery/e7-lenses-conv-2f.png`, y 565–635).
  Some text is stale for the mode: in Diffraction's *Rayleigh* mode the Setup subtitle still says
  "Narrow the slit and the pattern spreads", and the help paragraph explains sinc² and gratings
  (`shots/busy-diffraction-rayleigh-1440.png`, y≈223 and y 650–770). The hint bar lists shortcuts
  ("1–9 labs · [ ] exams") that nobody will read. Over a bright scene it's **2.35:1** contrast, and over
  the Gauss sphere it runs straight across the yellow (`shots/first-load-1440.png`, y≈877).
- **Why it matters.** Every extra sentence competes with the one that matters. Duplicates teach students
  to skip text, including the one sentence that explains the answer.
- **Fix.** Delete the hint bar. Put shortcuts in a "?" popover and show "Drag to orbit" once as a fading
  coach mark. Delete the Setup subtitle (the lab's `hint`). Collapse Setup help prose behind "How to read
  this ▸". Cap the insight at about 40 words plus the displayed equation, with "More ▸". Make the
  magforce key a real legend.
- **Effort.** S

### 9. The problem flow stalls on small things, several per problem
- **What's wrong.**
  - The worked example sits *directly above* the symbol box and ends in the exact formula the student is
    asked to type next: kQ/√(a²+y²) in the example (`shots/problem-symbol-box-1440.png`, x 155–215,
    y 345–385), then the input at y 572–604. The step is copying. Right after the principle step, the
    example fills the panel to its bottom edge and the input is entirely below the fold
    (`shots/problem-worked-example-1440.png`, example at y 565–835).
  - Mid-typing, the parser shouts **"Unexpected end"** in orange (`shots/problem-symbol-typing-1440.png`,
    y≈702) while the student is still typing a normal fraction.
  - The symbol keys offer ε₀ and μ₀ for problems that use neither (a ring's V; a sphere's speed through
    ΔV), and they wrap unevenly: "√ ( )" alone on row 2 (`shots/problem-symbol-box-1440.png`, y 645–670),
    and "² √ ( )" on row 2 in the exam, splitting the operators across rows
    (`shots/exam-problem-2-1280.png`, x 33–165, y 618–642).
  - One number appears in **three formats** on the solved screen: 45.25 kV (scene label and readout),
    45200 V (lab row and solution) and the typed 45250 (`shots/problem-solved-labvals-1440.png`).
  - "What went wrong?" is five pills stacked one per line (`shots/missed-what-went-wrong-1440.png`,
    y 375–515).
  - "Practise" (button) sits next to "Practice" (everywhere else) (`shots/missed-weak-list-1440.png`,
    x 305–372, y 453–520).
- **Why it matters.** Each of these costs seconds and a bit of trust, several times per problem.
- **Fix.** Put the worked example in a collapsible panel *beside* or *above the problem statement*,
  collapsed to "Worked example (same kind, other numbers) ▸" once the principle is named, so the input
  follows the question directly. Suppress parse errors until the student pauses for 800 ms or leaves the
  box. Show only the problem's own symbols plus operators, one row, and put the rest behind "more". Format
  numbers the same everywhere (engineering prefix, 3–4 significant figures). Lay the gap choices out as a
  2×2 grid plus one. Standardize on "Practice".
- **Effort.** S–M

### 10. On a phone, the labs get a strip of scene between two shelves
- **What's wrong.** `shots/first-load-390.png`: the **Setup / Equations / Hide** switch shares a
  horizontally scrolling strip with scene toggles, so panel navigation and scene options look alike, and
  "E arrows" is cut at the right edge. The Setup sheet is 203 px (24 dvh) tall and shows four controls.
  Predict is out of reach (problem 4). The readout's third cell peeks in at x≈380 with no scroll
  affordance. The camera keeps desktop framing: the Gauss sphere overflows the screen,
  `shots/phone-e3-breakdown-390.png` shows a giant orange sphere cut off on the left with the doorknob off
  screen, `shots/phone-circuits-sheet-setup-390.png` clips the R₃ label at the right edge (x≈330–390,
  y≈400–440), and **Hide** leaves the circuit small in a mostly empty 650-px screen
  (`shots/phone-circuits-sheet-hide-390.png`). The lab tab strip is cut mid-word ("Powe",
  "Mag forc", "egrals").
- **Why it matters.** `mobile.css` states the right premise (practice first on a phone), but the first
  screen a phone user sees is still a lab.
- **Fix.** On a phone, land on the Practice list. Present labs as a **viewer**: full-width scene framed for
  portrait, one primary control, a "Values" pull-up for the equation panel, and no Setup sheet by default.
  Move Setup / Equations / Hide into the sheet's own header as a segmented control, separate from the scene
  toggles. Give each lab a portrait camera preset.
- **Effort.** M

## 3. Region by region

Numbers are at 1440×900 unless noted. "Words" counts the rendered text (math counts as text), measured
with the same Playwright session.

### 3.1 Header (brand, exam tabs, lab tabs, Practice, Units, stat)
- **Contents.** A logo, "FLUX", "PHY 2049 · Exam 2 · Ch 36–37", eight numeral tabs, 2–6 lab tabs,
  **Practice** + kbd "P", **Units** + kbd "U", and "46 problems · 0 mastered" (`shots/first-load-1440.png`,
  x 16–388, y 16–212). About 30 words. It's 196 px tall, or 214 px in Exam 4, and 366–396 px wide depending
  on the exam's lab names.
- **Its job, in one sentence:** "Where am I, and where can I go?" It also tries to be the entry to Practice
  and Units and a progress summary, which is three jobs.
- **Problems.** It's the tallest element on screen and says the least. The numerals carry no meaning. The
  kbd chips "P" and "U" take space in the most prominent buttons. The stat line is tiny (11 px) and
  mostly zeros. "Exam 2" appears both in the subtitle and as the highlighted tab.
- **Keep:** the logo, the lab tabs. **Cut:** the kbd chips (put them in tooltips), the subtitle's "Exam N"
  (the picker says it). **Merge:** exam tabs + subtitle into one picker, "Exam 2 · Fields & Gauss · Fri
  9/11 ▾". **Move:** Practice becomes a top-level `Explore | Practice` switch, and Units goes into the
  equation panel's header as a small "Units" link (it's reference for that panel). **Collapse:** the
  progress stat into the Practice switch's badge ("Practice · 3 due").

### 3.2 Setup panel
- **Contents (Gauss).** Title, subtitle hint, Scenario select, the five-button Gaussian surface group, a
  radius slider with its value and a typed box, **Play ∮E·dA**, +Charge / −Charge / Delete, a charge row
  (q, μC, "in" badge), a coordinate grid (q₁, P × x, y, z), "1 square = 12.5 cm", and the Predict card
  (`shots/first-load-1440.png`, x 1125–1423, y 197–835). **28 controls** in Gauss, 27 in Mag force and
  24 in Diffraction.
- **Its job:** "Change the setup." Its secondary jobs (Predict, help prose, a legend written in sentences)
  are what push it past the fold.
- **Problems.** The Scenario select sits *above* the mode buttons that switch between families of
  scenarios. Diffraction has both a "Single slit / Grating / Resolution" group and a scenario list
  that already implies the mode (`shots/busy-diffraction-rayleigh-1440.png`, y 280–352). Scenario names
  truncate in the select: "Double slit · 600 nm, d = 0.2 mm, l" and "Compound microscope — image at"
  (`shots/busy-interference-P-1440.png`, y≈311; `shots/busy-lenses-micro-1440.png`, y≈295). Circuits
  sliders have **no typed box**, unlike every other lab (the README says every slider does), and in the
  three-branch layout they're in scrambled order: R₄, ε₁, R₁, ε₂, R₃, ε₃, R₂
  (`shots/busy-circuits-threebranch-1440.png`, y 330–665). Mag force's speed box shows "100000" for
  1×10⁷ m/s: the number is cut off by a 62-px box (`shots/busy-magforce-helix-1440.png`, x 1358–1418,
  y≈396). Checkbox labels start lowercase with parentheticals ("each slit has width a = d/3 (adds the
  diffraction envelope)", `shots/busy-interference-P-1440.png`, y 570–598).
- **Keep:** Scenario, sliders with typed boxes, the charge and coordinate grid. **Cut:** the subtitle hint
  (it's repeated in the hint bar). **Collapse:** help prose → "How to read this ▸". Coordinates → "Positions
  ▸" (the drag already sets them, and students need them only when copying a problem). **Move:** Predict
  to the top (problem 4). The Mag force color key goes to a legend. **Merge:** mode buttons + Scenario into
  one grouped select, or show modes as tabs *above* Scenario. **Fix:** Circuits' boxes and slider order.

### 3.3 Equation panel (law, live rows, insight, mini-plot)
- **Contents.** The law (1–3 lines of display math), 6–13 live rows, the insight (a yellow title, 48–191
  words, a displayed equation) and sometimes a mini-plot (`shots/busy-potential-default-1440.png`,
  x 16–376, y 226–720).
- **Its job:** "Show where the number comes from." That's the app's best idea, and it's in the right place,
  next to the scene.
- **Problems.** The rows are a flat list with no hierarchy: inputs (κ, V), intermediates (C₀) and results
  (U) all have the same weight (`shots/capacitor-default-1280.png`, y 350–545). When a slider moves,
  nothing shows *which* row changed. The insight is often cut off by the panel bottom with only a fade to
  show it (`shots/capacitor-default-1280.png`: "Power supply holds V fixed" is the last visible line,
  y≈581). The mini-plot is clipped at the bottom and has no axis values
  (`shots/busy-potential-default-1440.png`, y 650–720). The Circuits law line is broken in the
  three-branch layout: "junction: I₁ + I + I₂ =" (`shots/busy-circuits-threebranch-1280.png`, y≈270).
  The insight mixes KaTeX with Unicode math: "I₃ = −461.54 mA" in sans next to typeset I₃
  (`shots/busy-circuits-twoloop-1440.png`, y≈668).

## 4. Flow maps

### 4.1 Open a lab, change the scenario, drag something, understand what changed (1440)
1. Find the exam: hover numerals to learn which is which (1–8 hovers), then click.
2. Click a lab tab.
3. Eye to the far right: Setup's Scenario select (x≈1270, y≈284). Pick one.
4. Eye to the center: the scene reframes.
5. Drag a charge (center).
6. Eye to the far left: which of the 6–13 live rows changed? Nothing highlights (x 33–359).
7. Eye to the bottom center: the readout, where the same numbers repeat, possibly truncated.
8. Eye back to the right: the coordinate boxes updated.

**Path:** right → center → left → bottom → right. That's about 1,100 px per cause→effect loop at 1440
(`shots/first-load-1440.png`), and a zig-zag of four regions.

**Friction:** no change highlight (step 6). Duplicate numbers (step 7). A first-time user doesn't know the
charge is draggable until reading the subtitle or the hint bar.

**Shorter path:** controls and equations in **one left column** (Setup under the equation panel, or as
tabs in it), so cause and effect are 200 px apart. Rows that change flash. The scene gets the rest of
the screen. The drag affordance is a pulsing ring on the draggable object for the first visit.

### 4.2 Predict first, start to finish (1280)
1. Notice the card, which shows only its header at the bottom of Setup (`shots/capacitor-default-1280.png`,
   y≈640). **Stall:** most students won't.
2. Scroll Setup, and the sliders scroll away (`shots/predict-before-1280.png`).
3. "Try it" (if shown) loads a preset and **silently replaces your setup**.
4. Answer 1–3 rows (3–15 chips to read).
5. Make the change.
6. Read before→after inside the card (`shots/predict-after-1280.png`). The scene change is small, and the
   control that changed is off screen.
7. Undo, or Next ›.

**Clicks:** 1 scroll + 1 (Try it) + 1–3 + 1 + 1 = 5–7. **Lost place:** step 2 (the Setup scroll hides the
setup), step 3 (a silent reset). **Shorter:** the card on top, expanded on first visit. "Try it" says what
it will load ("Loads: battery connected, d = 5 cm"). The changed control is highlighted, and the change is
labeled in the scene.

### 4.3 A practice problem, start to finish (1440)
1. Practice (header pill), or P.
2. The list: past three chip rows and a practice exam button (`shots/practice-list-chapter-1440.png`),
   scroll to the problem.
3. Click. The lab switches, sometimes to another exam, and the header tab moves.
4. Principle: four options + "Not sure" (`shots/problem-principle-step-1440.png`).
5. The worked example appears and pushes the input below the fold
   (`shots/problem-worked-example-1440.png`). **Scroll.**
6. The symbol box. Type, see the preview and units (`shots/problem-symbol-typed-1440.png`). "Unexpected end"
   flashes mid-typing (`shots/problem-symbol-typing-1440.png`).
7. The numeric box unlocks. Type.
8. **Check** (compete with Play ∫ dV on the right).
9. Solved: "Solved, with a worked example beside you…", with the lab value under the answer
   (`shots/problem-solved-labvals-1440.png`). **Scroll** for the solution.
10. "The lab checks you": the readout comes back, but **the equation panel stays hidden while Practice is
    open**, so the lab's explanation is missing exactly when the lab is "live"
    (`shots/problem-solved-top-1440.png`: no equation panel on the left).
11. Edit a slider. The "Reset to the problem" banner appears at the *top* of the sheet, which is now scrolled
    out of view. **Scroll up.**
12. "More problems" or "Same problem, new numbers" (`shots/problem-solved-end-1440.png`).

**Count:** about 9 clicks, 3–4 scrolls, one surprise lab/exam switch. **Where the lab and the sheet fight:**
steps 8 and 10. **Shorter:** the input directly under the statement, the worked example collapsed beside
it, and one primary button on screen. After solving, the sheet shows a compact "Lab: 45.2 kV ✓" row and
an **Explore in the lab** button that opens the lab controls and the equation panel together, with
"Reset to the problem" pinned to the sheet's sticky header.

### 4.4 Take a practice exam and review the results (1280)
1. Practice → **Practice exam · 8 problems · 50 min** (the largest button in the panel).
2. The timer and 1–8 pager (`shots/exam-in-progress-1280.png`). The lab is live and, for this problem,
   irrelevant, with grey veil boxes everywhere.
3. Answer, then Next →. Problem 2 is about a sphere accelerated through ΔV, but the scene stays on the
   point-charge setup with its grey veils (`shots/exam-problem-2-1280.png`).
4. **Submit exam** sits where Close sits on every other practice screen (top right of the sheet), then a
   **native confirm()** dialog.
5. Results: "Score 0%", a chapter table and a problem list (`shots/exam-results-1440.png`). The Predict card
   reappears in Setup, next to the exam results (`shots/exam-results-1440.png`, x 1139–1409, y 755–830).
6. Open one: the worked solution, then "Back to results".

**Stalls:** step 2 (the sandbox beside a timed exam), step 4 (Submit in Close's place, a system
dialog), step 5 (no "Review the ones I missed" action; the list shows red dots but no batch review).
**Shorter:** a focus mode (the sheet centered, the scene only when the problem has one, no Setup). Submit
at the end of the pager with an in-app confirm sheet. Results lead with **Review missed (N) →**.

### 4.5 A Review-due session that crosses exams (1440)
Seeded with one missed problem from each of Exams 2, 4, 6 and Wave optics, all due.
1. Practice. **Review due · 4** is outlined yellow, the same size as Mixed set, third in line under the
   practice exam button (`shots/review-due-list-1440.png`, x 205–383, y 369–405). The header stat now says
   "· 4 due" in 11-px text (y≈184).
2. Click. The first problem loads **and the whole app switches exam and lab**: the header goes to Exam 2 ·
   Field, the lab tabs change, and Setup is replaced (`shots/review-due-1-1440.png`). The only sign of the
   session is the back link "← Review · 1 of 4" (x 33–127, y≈251).
3. Principle, parts, Check or Show solution, then **Next in review due →**.
4. Problem 2 is Exam 4 · Circuits (`shots/review-due-2-1440.png`). The header grows by two lines because
   "Biot–Savart" wraps, and the sheet's top drops 17 px, so everything the eye had anchored moves. Problem 3
   is Exam 6 · EM wave (`shots/review-due-3-1440.png`): the header jumps again and a spectrum ruler appears.

**Lost place:** three exam and lab changes in three problems, each re-laying the header, and the exam tabs
stay clickable. One click on "2" to "go back" leaves the session with no warning. **Shorter:** a session
bar at the top of the sheet ("Review · 2 of 4 · from Exam 4: Circuits"), a fixed-height header, and exam
navigation dimmed while a session owns it (or a "Leave review?" prompt).

### 4.6 All of the above on a phone (390×844)
- **First screen:** a lab (`shots/first-load-390.png`). Practice is a pill in the header. **Should be:** the
  Practice list.
- **Lab:** Setup / Equations / Hide in the toggle strip. Setup's 203-px sheet. Predict unreachable
  without scrolling that sheet to y=984 (`shots/predict-before-390.png` shows it after scrolling: the rest
  of Setup is gone, and "Make the change" is below the sheet's fold).
- **Practice list:** good. Full-screen, 44-px rows (`shots/practice-list-chapter-390.png`). But the lab

- **Problem:** good. The sticky "← Problems · Show lab · ×" header, 40-px symbol keys, 16-px inputs
  (`shots/problem-symbol-typed-390.png`). **Show lab** gives a 350-px strip of scene with grey veil boxes
  and P's label cut at the top (`shots/phone-show-lab-390.png`).
- **Exam:** `shots/exam-in-progress-390.png`. The timer and pager fit, and the lab isn't shown. That's the

## 5. Consistency audit

### 5.1 Color → every meaning it has

| Color | UI meanings | Scene meanings |
|---|---|---|
| **Yellow #F4D345** | Practice & Units buttons; active exam tab; active lab underline; slider thumbs; primary buttons (Play, Check, Make the change, Try it, Practice exam, More problems); insight titles; Predict title; pressed prediction chip; principle-step border + title; hint text; solution heading; "Review due" button; selected charge row border; "Your turn" guide text; focus ring; `.warn` values | current (Biot wire, Ampère wires, Faraday loop, Lenz chevrons, circuit/RC dots, bulb filament, AC I phasor); E at the probe; net E from a distribution; capacitor E arrows; outward flux patches and E·n̂ > 0 arrows (Gauss); selected charge ring & label; selected force; vector angle arc; polarizer beam; intermediate image (lenses); spark; interference/thin film "sum" wave |
| **Blue #58C4DD** | active toggle/chip fill; links; worked-example box; Units symbols; "here" lab badge; learning status dot; "Guided" / "Review from earlier" tags; highlighted equation values (Δy, θ_min, t_bright); `qV` voltage class | negative charge; − plate; inward flux; vector **b**; lens outlines; microscope final image; battery; low end of wire potential; V phasor |
| **Red #FC6255** | wrong / bad / missed; danger; timer < 5 min; "Principles you most often miss" box; `qP` power class; +Charge button | positive charge; + plate; vector **a**; ring/rod charge (Integrals); Mag force orbit path; real image arrow (lenses); breakdown E; high end of equipotential ramp |
| **Green #83C167** | correct; mastered; "in" badge; "agrees"; units ✓; readout `ok` = headline value (Δy, d, θ_min), no breakdown, near resonance, real image, visible band, inside metal; `qR` resistance | resistors; n̂ normals (Gauss); field-line ramp middle; visible band |
| **Teal #5CD0B3** | `qB` | B field (Mag force, EM wave); refracted ray; slit-1 / off-top wave; source 2 (Rayleigh) |
| **Gold #F0AC5F** | parse warnings; "differs"; `qC` capacitance | point A (Potential); force arrows (Force lab); F (Mag force); incident ray; object arrow; off-bottom wave; VC phasor |

**Proposed semantic palette.** *Status* (UI only, never in the scene): green = right, red = wrong,
amber = warning. *Action:* yellow = the single primary button on screen, and nothing else in the UI (active
tabs and toggles go neutral white-on-grey). *Physics* (the scene, plus the same quantity's value in the
equation panel): E = yellow, B = teal, I = gold dots, F = orange, v = white; + charge = red, − charge =
blue. *Geometry and optics* (rays, images, vectors a/b) use the remaining hues (purple, pink, light blue)
and never red, green or yellow. Scalar fields keep their ramps, with a CVD-safe ramp as the default.
That still leaves yellow meaning both "the primary button" and "E", but one is always a panel control and
the other always in the scene, which is a separation a student can learn.

**Double meanings that collide on one screen:** yellow "press this" and yellow "current" on Circuits
(`shots/busy-circuits-twoloop-1440.png`: yellow Try it, yellow I labels, yellow dots). Green "correct" and
green "resistor" on Circuits (green R zigzags next to green "= 0 ✓" sums). Blue "negative charge" and blue
"active toggle" on Gauss. Red "wrong" and red "positive charge" on every charge lab in practice.

### 5.2 Type scale inventory
- **20 fixed pixel sizes:** 8, 9.5, 10, 10.5, 11, 11.5, 12, 12.5, 13, 13.5, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20,
  22, 24, and 3 `clamp()`s, plus 9 em-relative KaTeX adjustments (0.72–1.32 em). 11 px alone is used 29
  times. The steps between 10 and 15 are half-pixels, which no one can tell apart.
- **Weights:** 400, 500 and 600, with no rule. Setup's "Setup" is serif 16/400, the Practice title is serif
  20/400, and the principle name is sans 600.
- **Families:** Inter (UI), the KaTeX serif (values, headings, readout values, problem text, buttons like
  Practice and Check), KaTeX math, and JetBrains Mono (symbol input and op keys only). **Mono is used
  well:** only where characters must be distinct (`k*Q/sqrt(...)`). The serif is overloaded. It's the
  math face *and* the heading face *and* the button face on some buttons (Practice, Check, Play,
  Practice exam) but not others (Mixed set, Hint, Show solution), so two buttons in one row use different
  fonts (`shots/problem-symbol-box-1440.png`, y≈806: serif "Check" next to sans "Hint · 1 left").
- **Proposal:** five sizes: 11 (meta), 13 (body/UI), 15 (problem text, values), 18 (panel titles), 24
  (headline number/timer). Serif for math and problem statements only. Buttons all in Inter.

### 5.4 Spacing and alignment
- Gaps use 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 and 12 px. Paddings use more than 30 distinct values. There's no scale.
- Panel tops don't align. Setup's top is a fixed 196, while the equation panel's top follows the header
  (225, or 242 in Exam 4), so the two columns start 29–46 px apart (`coverage.json`;
  `shots/busy-circuits-twoloop-1440.png`: equation panel y=242, Setup y=196).
- The brand panel's width changes per exam (366–396 px, measured) because the lab tabs size it, so the
  header's right edge moves when you switch exams (`shots/first-load-1440.png` x=388 vs
  `shots/capacitor-default-1280.png` x=412).
- At 1024 the readout's labels wrap and it grows from 65 to 81–114 px tall, depending on the lab
  (`coverage.json`).
- **Proposal:** a 4-px scale (4, 8, 12, 16, 24), a fixed header height, and one column width.

### 5.5 KaTeX next to UI text
- Readout labels mix sans ("Battery 1 current") and italic KaTeX ("C", "U") in the same row
  (`shots/busy-circuits-twoloop-1440.png`, y≈810; `shots/capacitor-default-1280.png`, y≈630).
- Setup labels are sans with KaTeX subscripts ("Battery ε₁"), while its values are serif ("12.00 V")
  and its typed boxes are sans ("12").
- The Setup subtitle and hint bar show math as plain text ("F = q v × B — the sign of × is the whole
  point", `shots/busy-magforce-helix-1440.png`, y≈223 and y≈877), while the equation panel typesets the
  same law.
- Insight prose uses Unicode subscripts in places ("I₃ = −461.54 mA") and KaTeX in others.
- Minus signs: "-14.0" (hyphen) and "−25.0 cm" (true minus) in the same readout
  (`shots/busy-lenses-micro-1440.png`, y≈833).

### 5.6 Contrast and focus
Computed with the WCAG formula, with the panel alpha composited over the scene behind it:
- On panels, `--text-muted` #aaa39e is 7.7:1 over black and 6.0:1 over the yellow sphere. `--text-dim`
  #8f8983 is 5.5:1 over black but **4.2–4.3:1 over a bright scene**, under AA for its 10.5–11-px uses (the
  Setup subtitle, legend ends, Setup help prose).
- The hint bar (no panel) is **2.35:1** over the Gauss sphere and 2.8:1 over an axis line.
- Grid tick labels have a black outline, which keeps them legible, but the text itself is 1.7:1 over the
  sphere.
- The locked numeric placeholder is **about 2.0:1**. A disabled button (Review due · 0) is 3.3:1.
- Status colors are fine on panels (red 5.0–6.4:1, green 7–8.9:1).
- **Focus:** a visible 2-px yellow outline with an offset (`shots/focus-tab-1-1440.png`, the "1" exam tab
  at x 27–79, y 84–116). That's good. **Tab order** in Capacitor (measured): stops 1–8 are the exam
  numerals, 9–14 the lab tabs, 15 Practice, 16 Units, 17 Reset view (jumping to the top right corner),
  and only **stop 18** reaches the first Setup control. Stops 29–32 are the Predict card, then focus falls
  to `<body>` and wraps. There's no skip link and no landmark jump, so a keyboard user presses Tab 17 times
  per lab to reach a slider. The scene can't be operated by keyboard (no way to move a charge or the
  probe), though the typed coordinate boxes are an alternative.

## 6. Proposed layout

### 6.1 Laptop (≥1024): two docked columns, scene in the middle, framed into the free rectangle


In **Practice** mode the left column becomes the problem sheet (480 px). The equation/setup content moves
into a closed **Lab** drawer at the sheet's foot, which opens after solving. The scene stays as the viewer.

**Reasoning.** Cause (slider) and effect (row, result) live in one column, 200 px apart, with no
zig-zag. The scene gets a real rectangle and the camera frames *into* it, which fixes problem 3 at the
root. The readout and the hint bar go away. Predict sits where the eye already is. The header becomes
fixed-height and never shifts.

**Arguing against it.**
- It loses the "floating over a cinematic scene" look that makes FLUX feel like 3Blue1Brown. Docked
  columns look like an IDE. *Mitigation:* keep the translucent panels and blur, and just stop floating
  them independently.
- One left column at 1024×768 is 360 × 720 px for the law, results, predict, setup and why. That's a lot of
  scrolling, and the thing this proposal complains about (Predict below the fold) could return for Setup
  instead. *Mitigation:* tabs inside the column ("Equations | Setup") at under 900 px of height, which is
  another level of hierarchy.
- Framing the scene with a view offset means the canvas center moves between Explore and Practice
  modes. Labs that lock rotation and draw schematics (Circuits, Lenses) need their fit logic reworked for a
  narrower rectangle, and that's the riskiest engineering here.
- Moving Setup to the left breaks every returning student's muscle memory.

### 6.2 Alternative (smaller change): keep the corners, fix the collisions

```
┌─────────────────┐                                              ┌────────────────┐
│ FLUX  Exam 4 ▾  │                                              │ ● ○ ○  ⟲  ?    │
│ Circuits RC ... │                                              └────────────────┘
│ Explore|Practice│
├─────────────────┤                                              ┌────────────────┐
│ LAW             │                                              │ ▸ Predict …    │
│ RESULT  big     │              scene (view-offset to           │ SETUP          │
│ rows (flash)    │               the gap between columns)       │ …              │
│ ▸ Why           │                                              │ ▸ How to read  │
└─────────────────┘                                              └────────────────┘
   (no readout, no hint bar, legend inside the View card top right)
```

**Why:** it's the cheapest change that removes the readout, the hint bar, the header shift and the Predict
fold, and frames the camera. **Against:** the cause→effect path stays a left↔right zig-zag of about 1,000
px, and Practice is still a column that shares the stage.

## 8. Open questions for the builder

1. **Is FLUX primarily a practice tool with labs attached, or a lab with practice attached?** The answer
   decides between §6.1's `Explore | Practice` switch and keeping Practice as a panel.
2. **Should a timed practice exam show a lab at all?** A real exam doesn't. If the answer is "only as a
   diagram", the exam can drop Setup entirely.
3. **Do students navigate by exam number, or by date and topic?** If the syllabus says "Exam 4", numerals
   are fine *with* names. If students think "Friday's exam", default to the next exam date.
4. **Is the readout there for projection in lecture** (large numbers visible from the back of a room)? If so,
   make it a "Present" mode instead of always-on.
5. **Is the worked example meant to contain the exact formula the student types next?** If fading is the
   goal, the first-exposure example could stop one step short of the symbolic answer.
6. **Is the 3D orbit important in the 2D labs** (Circuits, Lenses, Refraction, AC), where rotation is locked
   and the grid is decoration? Those labs could drop the number plane and frame tighter.
7. **Who uses the keyboard shortcuts?** If it's only the builder, the hint bar can go today.
8. **Is color-blind safety a requirement?** Circuits' wire ramp is already CVD-safe (`legend-cvd`), and
   grading always pairs color with ✓/✗ text, which holds up. The field-line ramp (blue→green→yellow→red,
   `shots/busy-field-default-1440.png`) and the equipotential ramp (`shots/busy-potential-default-1440.png`)
   put green and red at adjacent steps with no second cue. If CVD safety is a goal, those two ramps are the
   ones to swap.
9. **Is "29 labs" the intended count?** The catalog has 26 labs across 27 tab slots (Integrals twice).
